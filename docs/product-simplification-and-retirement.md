# OnScreenSync product simplification and retirement candidates

Prioritise a clear path from pairing a TV to publishing a screen. Remove confusing public claims and duplicate choices first. Retire existing playback capabilities only after identifying affected customers and verifying that the replacement preserves the content they rely on.

These are recommendations, based on repository evidence and public signage documentation. There is no usage or customer interview evidence in this review that establishes an existing feature is unwanted.

## Keep the workflows customers build screens around

Retain uploads, looping video, images, rich text, menus, clocks, playlists, device pairing, and publishing. A clock is simple but useful in receptions and shared spaces. Keep partner and tenant administration separate from trial onboarding, and reserve remote restart, termination, and upgrade actions for authorised support roles. Public examples from Anthias and Screenly reinforce the ongoing role of asset libraries, scheduling, and playlists. They do not show demand for any particular OnScreenSync implementation. [Anthias](https://github.com/Screenly/Anthias) · [Screenly CLI](https://github.com/Screenly/CLI)

## How to make a retirement decision

1. Count active screens and tenants that use the feature over at least one normal content cycle. Check saved configurations as well as new usage, and review support cases and partner commitments.
2. Speak with affected customers, especially those with long-running screens or seasonal campaigns. Confirm that the feature can be retired, moved out of the default workflow, or replaced without loss of content.
3. Test a replacement on each affected player platform. Compare screenshots or played content as appropriate. Preserve saved data and provide a way to revert the change if output differs.
4. For supported features with active users, pause new adoption first and give affected customers a migration window. Check for remaining use before removing playback support or stored data.

Research reviewed on 18 November 2026. Public repositories were used for competitive context and for tracing current OnScreenSync behaviour.
